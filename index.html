About

I am a Ph.D. student in Political Science at the University of Pennsylvania, where I am also completing an M.A. in Statistics and Data Science at the Wharton School. My research is in urban political economy and comparative politics, with a focus on Latin America.

I study how cities change, through migration, capital inflows, infrastructure investment, and other transformations of urban space, and how those changes reshape political relationships among residents, social groups, and the state. I treat the built environment as a mechanism that structures politics: how urban space is organized and altered shapes who comes into contact with whom, which local resources become contested, and what the state can see and respond to. To answer these questions, I use quantitative methods and large-scale observational data, including administrative and geospatial records.

Before Penn, I earned a B.A. in Political Science from the Instituto Tecnológico Autónomo de México (ITAM) and worked in public policy consulting in Mexico City. My graduate studies are supported by a SECIHTI fellowship and a Benjamin Franklin Fellowship.
